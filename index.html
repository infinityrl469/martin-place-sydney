What is Martin Place?

Martin Place is a famous pedestrian public space in the centre of Sydney, in the state of New South Wales. It stretches between George Street and Macquarie Street, surrounded by historic buildings, offices, cafés and cultural landmarks.

It is more than a street: it is a meeting place for residents, workers and visitors. People come here to admire the architecture, take a break, attend public events and remember important moments in Australian history.

From a lane to a landmark

A story through time

Martin Place developed alongside Sydney's growth. Its name honours Sir James Martin, a former Premier and Chief Justice of New South Wales.

1860s–1892
The area beside the General Post Office developed from a narrow lane into a wider public street. Martin Place officially opened in September 1892.
1921–1935
The street was extended eastwards. By 1935, it connected George Street with Macquarie Street.
1929
The Sydney Cenotaph was unveiled as a memorial to Australians who died in service during the First World War.
1971–1979
Sections were progressively closed to traffic, creating the pedestrian place visitors see today.
See and discover

Landmarks not to miss

Look around and you will find a mix of heritage architecture, public art and places that tell the story of Sydney.

The General Post Office (GPO)

This grand sandstone building is one of Martin Place's best-known landmarks. Its clock tower and detailed façade make it a highlight for architecture lovers.

The Sydney Cenotaph

Unveiled in 1929, this granite memorial honours Australians who died in service during the First World War. A soldier and a sailor stand beside it.

Historic architecture

Martin Place is lined with impressive heritage façades and modern office buildings. Look for stonework, columns, arches and decorative details.

Did you know?

Three interesting facts

Its name has a story

Martin Place is named after Sir James Martin, who served as Premier and Chief Justice of New South Wales.

A place for remembrance

The Cenotaph is an important focus for commemorative ceremonies, including Anzac Day services.

A lively public space

Today, the boulevard is used for everyday city life as well as major public events and celebrations.

On Gadigal Country

Martin Place is on the traditional lands of the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation. Their connection to the area goes back long before the modern city.
